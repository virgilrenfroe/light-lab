White is a mix

Light. Red, green, and blue are up. Where all three beams meet, the card is white.

White light is a mix of colors. The primary colors of light are red, green, and blue. On a stage those lamps add. Red and green overlap as yellow. Green and blue make cyan. Blue and red make magenta. Where all three meet, the card goes white. On paper, pigments subtract. The primary colors of these pigments are cyan, magenta, and yellow. Cyan ink removes red light. Magenta removes green. Yellow removes blue. Where two inks overlap, only one color of light remains, and the page is darker. Where all three overlap, almost no light comes back, and the page is near black.

Where you'll see this

A lighting designer, or a theatrical electrician, mixes red, green, and blue lamps on a stage. Where two beams cross, the colors add. Red and green make yellow. Green and blue make cyan. Blue and red make magenta. All three together make white. One set of lamps can paint many colors on the scenery.

A display engineer builds a screen from red, green, and blue subpixels. From a normal viewing distance the eye blends each trio into one color. When all three subpixels are full, that spot looks white. Turn one down and the white shifts. It is the same addition as the three lamps, in a tight grid.

A print technician, or a press operator, prints with cyan, magenta, yellow, and black ink. Cyan removes red light, magenta removes green, and yellow removes blue. Where those three overlap, the paper goes dark, but the dark is a muddy near-black, not a solid black. The black ink is there for type and for deep shadows, so the color inks do not have to carry that job alone.
