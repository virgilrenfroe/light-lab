Two reflections meet

A soap film. Watch which colors grow stronger, and which cancel.

Light reflects from the front and from the back. The back path is longer. That extra length is the path difference. If the waves line up, that color gets stronger. That is constructive interference. If the waves land out of step, that color cancels. That is destructive interference. Thickness picks the hue. A steeper view shifts the bands. The front reflection flips. The back one does not. Near zero thickness, every color cancels. The film looks dark. The top is thinner, so it goes dark first.

Where you'll see this

An optical engineer coats eyeglasses and camera lenses with a very thin film. The thickness is chosen so the reflection from the front and the reflection from the back cancel. Less light bounces off the lens, and more light goes through.

That coat often looks faintly purple or green. Those are the colors that do not fully cancel. A coating technician checks the tint to see that the film landed near the thickness the engineer asked for.

An environmental scientist reads the colors of a thin oil slick on water. Thickness decides which colors cancel, so the hue is a rough guide to how thick the film is. A soap bubble works the same way. The dark patch is the thinnest part.
