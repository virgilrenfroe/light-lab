Split by wavelength

White light is many wavelengths. Watch how the prism sorts them.

White light holds many wavelengths. In glass, a shorter wavelength slows more, so it bends more. Violet bends more than red. The beam leaves as a spectrum. Crown glass spreads the colors a little. Flint glass spreads them more, and the fan opens. The index of refraction, n, says how hard the glass bends light. Turn the prism to change the angle of incidence. Past the critical angle, light inside the glass cannot leave the second face. It reflects. That is total internal reflection. Violet reaches that angle first. Red can still leave.

Where you'll see this

A chemist can send light from a hot sample through a prism. The spectrum breaks into bright lines. Each element has its own lines, so the chemist can tell what is in the sample.

An astronomer spreads starlight into a spectrum. Dark lines in that spectrum show which elements are in the star.

A network technician sends internet signals through a glass fiber. Past the critical angle, the light reflects inside the glass instead of leaking out. That is total internal reflection, and it keeps the signal in the cable.
