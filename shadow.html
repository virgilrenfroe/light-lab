The dark core and the grey fringe

The dark core is the umbra. No light from the lamp reaches it. The grey fringe is the penumbra. Part of the lamp is blocked there, and part is not.

A small lamp is nearly a point. Each place on the screen is either in the light or in the shadow, so the edge is sharp. A wide lamp has a size. From one place on the screen you may see the whole lamp, part of it, or none of it. The dark core is the umbra. The object covers the whole lamp, so no light from the lamp arrives. The grey fringe is the penumbra. Part of the lamp is blocked, and part still shines. A larger lamp makes that fringe wider, so the shadow looks soft.

Move the object closer to the screen and the fringe has less room to spread, so the edge sharpens. Move the screen farther away and the fringe grows. Where the object covers the whole lamp, the shadow is the same idea as a solar eclipse. The Moon covers the Sun. The dark core of that shadow on the Earth is the umbra, and the partial shadow around it is the penumbra.

Where you'll see this

A gaffer, or a cinematographer, softens a face with a large softbox. The softbox is a wide lamp, so it covers more of the view from the face. The shadow under the nose and the chin gets a wide penumbra, and the edge looks soft. A small bare bulb at the same distance is nearly a point. That same shadow edge goes sharp.

A photographer moves a subject farther from a wall to change the penumbra. The wall is the screen. With more space between the person and the wall, the grey fringe has room to spread, so the shadow looks softer. Move the person close to the wall and that fringe shrinks, so the edge looks sharper.

An astronomer, or a science teacher, uses these words for an eclipse. In a solar eclipse the Moon covers the Sun. Where the Moon hides the whole Sun, that place on the Earth is in the umbra, and the day goes dark. Around that path the Moon hides only part of the Sun. That partial shadow is the penumbra. In a lunar eclipse the Moon passes through the shadow the Earth casts, first the penumbra and then, in a total eclipse, the umbra.
